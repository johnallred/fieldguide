# Start here

**A curriculum for a job category that does not have a settled name yet.**

Three job postings started this. One from Intel, for a role in Developer
Relations that asks for golden paths, curriculum, documentation CI, cold-start
testing, and content optimized for SEO and GEO. One from NVIDIA, for something
it calls KnowledgeOps, which asks for content modeling, taxonomy, metadata for
machine retrieval, RAG packaging, agent skills, and evaluation sets for answer
accuracy. One from Anthropic, for a role called Developer Education Lead that asks for demos, labs, quick-reference guides, technical currciulum, self-service resources, experimental tools, content-freshness automation, etc.

The titles are different. The work is essentially same work, and it is a real
discipline with no agreed curriculum, no textbook, and almost no one
deliberately trained for it. This guide is an attempt at the curriculum.

**It covers 106 concepts.** Every one is here because one of those two postings
asks for it, and every one says which posting and in what words. The
[coverage matrix](coverage.md) prints the receipts, including the places where
the claim is this author's argument rather than a quotation.

## What a concept page looks like

Every one answers the same seven questions in the same order:

1. **What it is**, in two sentences, for someone who has heard the term in a
   meeting and not admitted they do not know it.
2. **Why the job asks for it**, connected to the posting line, explaining the
   work behind the words.
3. **The golden path**: the shortest real exercise that produces a working
   result, from a clean environment, ending in something observable.
4. **Reference**: the facts worth looking up later. Tables, not narrative.
5. **How to talk about it**, including the tradeoff, because reciting what a
   thing does is not the signal.
6. **How to prove it**: the artifact that demonstrates it without you having to
   claim it.
7. **Related**: the lateral links the prerequisite graph does not know about,
   each with a clause saying why a reader would follow it from here.

That consistency is not a stylistic preference. It is a
[content model](explanation/the-page-contract.md) applied to a learning corpus,
and CI rejects a page that violates it. The payoff is that the thirtieth page
costs you nothing to navigate, because you learned the shape on the first.

## This guide is also an example of its own subject

A guide about information architecture that was itself a folder of unstructured
Markdown would be an argument against its own content.

So the curriculum is a validated data file, `concepts.yaml`, and the pages that
describe it are generated from that file. A concept marked complete with no
page fails the build. A page nobody planned fails the build. A learning path
that teaches a prerequisite too late fails the build. A page missing one of its
seven sections fails the build.

Three of those four checks caught real mistakes in this repository within
minutes of being written, which is the honest argument for all of it. See
[why a registry](explanation/why-a-registry.md).

## Where to be sceptical

**The postings are not reproduced here.** Verbatim quotes were recorded while
reading them and are marked `quoted`. Everything marked `derived` is a
reconstruction of a requirement, and `adjacent` means neither posting mentions
it and this author thinks the named work is impossible without it. 90 of the 106 concepts are `quoted`. The [provenance page](explanation/provenance.md)
explains why that distinction is printed on every row rather than quietly
averaged away.

**One person wrote this.** The golden paths have been run; the career advice
is opinion. Where a page recommends a tool over an alternative, it says what
the alternative is better at.
