    Choosing a static site generator¶

    The decision is about how much correctness the build checks, not about which syntax you prefer.

    core · difficulty 2 · 3h

    Asked for by name

    NVIDIA: "static site generators (Sphinx, MkDocs, Docusaurus, or similar)"

    Prerequisites: Markdown and its dialects

    Leads to: MkDocs and Material, Sphinx, Docusaurus, Antora and AsciiDoc, Versioned documentation

    What it is¶

    The tool that turns a tree of source files into a website. It decides your Markdown dialect, your navigation model, your theming, your extension mechanism, and, least discussed and most important, how much of your corpus's correctness the build is willing to check.

    NVIDIA names three by name. The useful skill is not having a favourite, it is being able to say what each one's build does and does not verify.

    Why the job asks for it¶

    Because the choice is close to irreversible and it is usually made by whoever set up the repository in week one.

    Migrating a mature corpus between generators means rewriting every tool-specific construct, remapping every URL, rebuilding the navigation model and retraining contributors. People do not do it. So the generator you inherit is the generator you have, and the valuable thing is to know its blind spots well enough to close them with CI gates.

    That framing is also the honest answer to "which would you choose". The differences in syntax and theming are small and mostly taste. The differences in what the build catches are large and measurable.

    The golden path¶

    Build identical content in two generators and compare what each one is willing to fail on. About thirty-five minutes.

    Step 1: the same three defects in both

    mkdir -p ssg/mk/docs ssg/sx/src && cd ssg

# MkDocs
printf 'site_name: W\n' > mk/mkdocs.yml
cat > mk/docs/index.md <<'EOF'
# Widget

See [the tuning bit](guide.md#tuning).
EOF
printf '# Guide\n\n## Setup\n\nContent.\n' > mk/docs/guide.md
printf '# Orphan\n\nNobody links here.\n' > mk/docs/orphan.md

# Sphinx, with MyST so the source is also Markdown
cat > sx/src/conf.py <<'EOF'
project = "W"
extensions = ["myst_parser"]
EOF
cat > sx/src/index.md <<'EOF'
# Widget

See [the tuning bit](guide.md#tuning).

```{toctree}
guide
```
EOF
printf '# Guide\n\n## Setup\n\nContent.\n' > sx/src/guide.md
printf '# Orphan\n\nNobody links here.\n' > sx/src/orphan.md

    Two defects: a link to guide.md#tuning where guide.md has no Tuning heading, and a page in no navigation at all.

    Step 2: ask both builds, in their strictest mode

    ( cd mk && python3 -m mkdocs build --strict >/dev/null 2>&1; echo "mkdocs --strict exit=$?" )
sphinx-build -q -W -b html sx/src sx/out >/dev/null 2>&1; echo "sphinx -W  exit=$?"

    mkdocs --strict exit=0
sphinx -W  exit=1

    WARNING: local id not found in doc 'guide': 'tuning' [myst.xref_missing]
WARNING: document isn't included in any toctree [toc.not_included]

    MkDocs's strictest mode passes a corpus with a dead heading anchor and an unreachable page. Sphinx fails on both, and names them.

    This is the measured version of the gap that link checking exists to close: the custom anchor checker on that page is necessary on MkDocs and redundant on Sphinx.

    Step 3: measure what the stricter build costs

    121 generated pages, same content in both:

    mkdocs,  cold build          0.58 s
sphinx,  cold build          3.52 s
sphinx,  incremental rebuild 0.75 s

    Sphinx is roughly six times slower cold, because it builds an intermediate doctree that makes cross-reference resolution possible. That doctree is also what gives it incremental rebuilds, which MkDocs does not have.

    Step 4: find out what actually dominates build time

    Same generator, same 121 pages, two configurations:

    mkdocs, default theme, no extensions    0.6 s
mkdocs, Material theme, 12 extensions  16.0 s

    Twenty-seven times slower, with the generator held constant. Your theme and extension set matter far more to build time than your choice of generator. Anyone comparing generators on speed without holding the theme constant is measuring the wrong thing.

    What you learned. The real axis is how much the build verifies, the real cost of verification is a doctree and some seconds, and the headline speed numbers people quote are mostly about themes.

    Reference¶

    What each build checks, measured¶

    Defect MkDocs --strict Sphinx -W
    Link to a missing page Fails Fails
    Link to a missing heading anchor Passes Fails
    Page in no navigation Passes Fails
    Reference to a missing symbol Fails, with mkdocstrings autorefs Fails, with domains
    Duplicate label No such concept Fails

    The second and third rows are what you have to replace with your own gates on MkDocs. The fourth row needs a tool that collects symbols: Sphinx natively, or MkDocs with mkdocstrings and its [text][symbol] references, which are checked. Plain Markdown links to generated anchors are not.

    The four, in one table¶

    MkDocs Sphinx Docusaurus Antora
    Source Markdown RST, or Markdown via MyST MDX AsciiDoc
    Runtime Python Python Node Node
    Cross-reference checking Pages only Pages, anchors, symbols Pages Pages, across repos
    Versioning A plugin A convention plus builds Built in Built in, first-class
    Multi-repository No No No Yes, its reason to exist
    Interactive components No No Yes No
    Setup cost Lowest Medium Medium Highest
    Best at Getting good fast API correctness Product sites Many repos, one site

    Choosing, honestly¶

    Situation Choice
    A library's API docs, Python or C++ Sphinx
    A product site with interactive examples Docusaurus
    Documentation spread over many repositories Antora
    Anything else, and you want to be productive today MkDocs
    You inherited one Keep it, and close its gaps with gates

    The last row is the most common real situation and the best interview answer.

    What you will have to build yourself, by choice¶

    On MkDocs On Sphinx
    Anchor checking Little; the build covers it
    Orphan page detection Little
    Versioned docs, via a plugin A build-per-version convention
    A content model validator A content model validator
    Sample execution Sample execution

    Both still need sample validation and cold-start testing. No generator checks whether your quickstart works.

    How to talk about it¶

    Lead with correctness, not syntax. "The difference that matters is how much the build verifies. MkDocs in strict mode ships a dead heading anchor and an unreachable page; Sphinx with -W fails on both and also fails on a reference to a symbol that moved. So the choice is really about how much of the gate suite you are going to write yourself."

    The follow-up is usually about speed, and the honest answer corrects the premise. Sphinx is slower cold and has incremental rebuilds, but holding the generator constant and swapping a bare theme for Material and a dozen extensions cost twenty-seven times more than the generator choice did. Theme weight dominates.

    The tradeoff to volunteer. Sphinx's correctness comes from a doctree, and the doctree is also its difficulty: conf.py is a Python program, errors are reported in its own vocabulary, and contributors meet directives before they meet their first page. MkDocs is dramatically easier to adopt, and you pay for that later in gates you write yourself. Both are defensible; pretending one is simply better is the tell.

    One sharper point. Migration between generators effectively does not happen, so this reads like a high-stakes decision and mostly is not one: you will inherit a choice. The valuable skill is auditing what your generator's build does not check and closing those specific gaps, which is a concrete thing to have done and a much better interview answer than a preference.

    How to prove it¶

    The artifact. A table like step 2's for the generator you actually use, with the gaps you closed and how.

    Three things to do, in order:

    1. Run the golden path. Step 2 takes ten minutes and the result surprises most people who use MkDocs daily.
    2. Put a dead heading anchor into your own corpus and confirm whether your build catches it. If not, you have just identified your first gate.
    3. Time your build with your theme and again with the default theme. The ratio tells you where your build minutes actually go.

    What makes it credible rather than decorative. This guide runs on MkDocs with Material, and it has a hand-written anchor checker, a fence gate and a render gate precisely because the build does not cover them. Being able to say "I chose this generator and here are the three checks I added to compensate" is the answer that demonstrates judgment rather than preference.
