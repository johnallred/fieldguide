    Instructional design for developers¶

    The structural commitments that make a page teach rather than inform, and why auditing your own corpus for them is harder than writing them.

    core · difficulty 2 · 5h

    Asked for by name

    Intel: "Design and maintain golden paths and curriculum that take a developer from a clean environment to a working result"

    Leads to: Learning paths and curriculum, Cognitive load in technical content, Assessment and checks for understanding

    What it is¶

    The practice of designing content so that a reader can do something afterwards, rather than having read about it. For developer documentation that reduces to a small number of structural commitments: say what the reader will be able to do, say what they need first, give them something to practise on, and give them a way to know it worked.

    The reason this is not obvious is that documentation and instruction are different jobs sharing one medium. A reference page is finished when it is accurate. A tutorial is finished when a stranger reaches a working result, which is a claim about the reader rather than about the page.

    Why the job asks for it¶

    Intel's bullet is the concept in one line: "from a clean environment to a working result". That is an instructional objective written in behavioural terms, with its starting state named. The posting is describing design, not writing.

    It matters on an ordinary Tuesday because most developer documentation is written by people who already know the thing, and expertise is the enemy of instruction. An expert cannot see which of their steps are inferential leaps, having stopped making those leaps years ago. Structural commitments are the defence: if every page must declare its prerequisites, the author is forced to notice what they were assuming.

    The golden path¶

    Audit a corpus for four structural commitments, and find out what auditing your own writing actually costs. About thirty-five minutes.

    Step 1: four commitments, chosen because a script can check them

    Commitment In this corpus Checkable as
    Objective What the exercise produces A stated result at its end
    Entry state What the reader must already know A prerequisites line
    Practice Something to do, not to read The golden path itself
    Verification How the reader knows it worked A "how to prove it" section

    These are not the only possible commitments. They are four that a program can verify, which is what makes them worth adopting over good intentions.

    Step 2: audit the corpus

    for c in [x for x in reg.concepts if x.status == "complete"]:
    t = (docs / c.page).read_text()
    if c.prerequisites and "**Prerequisites:**" in t:   has["prereq"] += 1
    if DURATION.search(t):                              has["dur"] += 1
    if re.search(r"\*\*What you (?:built|learned)", t):  has["result"] += 1
    if "## How to prove it" in t:                       has["proof"] += 1

    complete pages: 82

  prereq stated      55/73   (75%)
  duration stated    75/82   (91%)
  closing result     78/82   (95%)
  proof section      82/82   (100%)

    (The audit below ran when the corpus was 82 pages. On the finished 106 the same four commitments stand at 89/89, 106/106, 106/106 and 106/106.)

    Two of those four numbers are wrong, and working out which is the real lesson of this page.

    Step 3: the prerequisite number was counting labels, not commitments

    prerequisite markers in use:
  **Prerequisites:**   46
  **Read first:**      28
concepts with no prerequisites at all: 9

    Coverage was already complete. The corpus was using two labels for one structural element: hand-written pages said "Prerequisites", pages that began in the scaffold said "Read first", because that is the string the scaffold emitted.

    A real defect, just not the one the number suggested. A reader meeting both labels does not infer two conventions, they experience inconsistency, and the entry-state commitment stops being recognisable as a commitment at all. Fixed by standardising on one label, changing the scaffold, and asserting it in a test so it cannot drift back.

    labels in use after the pass: {'Prerequisites:': 74}
prereq coverage: 73/73

    On the finished corpus: 90 occurrences, 89 of 89 concepts that have prerequisites. One page states the label twice, which is a cosmetic inconsistency the check does not care about and a reader would not notice.

    What this fix got wrong, which took a while to see. It standardised the label that had surfaced and asserted that one label in a test, and that is exactly as far as it went. Three more elements were carrying two wordings each at the time, in other sections, and the test written here could not have seen any of them, because it was a test about prerequisites rather than about labels.

    The assessment page found the second pair independently, months later, by measuring something else entirely. All four are standardised now, against STRUCTURAL_LABELS in registry.py rather than against a string written into a test. A fix applied to the instance is not applied to the class, and the way to tell the difference is whether the check you add afterwards could find a defect you had not already found.

    Step 4: the duration number was the line-wrapping bug

    Seven pages appeared to state no duration. All seven state one:

    docs/concepts/content-lifecycle.md:  "... falsifiable. About thirty
                                      minutes. This uses this guide's own ..."

    The number and its unit were separated by a line break, and the pattern's [- ]? does not match a newline. Normalising whitespace before searching:

    duration stated: 82/82

    This is the third time in this project that measuring wrapped prose per line has produced a confident wrong answer, which is worth more than the result.

    Step 5: count the real failures, then fix them

    With both measurements corrected, exactly one commitment was genuinely incomplete:

    golden paths with no stated result: 4/82
   diataxis, antora, github-actions, mcp

    Four exercises that ended without telling the reader what they now had. Writing those four closings took ten minutes, and each one forced the useful question: what did this exercise produce? On diataxis the answer came out sharper than the page's own introduction.

    golden paths with no stated result: 0/82

    Step 6: the finding worth carrying out of here

    Count the attempts. Across this corpus, eight successive audits of my own writing returned plausible wrong numbers:

    Attempt Wrong because
    Golden path structure Split sections on ##, which golden paths write inside heredocs
    Duration, first try Matched only "About N minutes"
    Duration, second try Matched per line, and prose wraps
    Duration, third try Word list omitted the hyphenated numbers
    Sentence length Front matter counted as prose
    Sentence length, again ." not treated as a sentence ending
    Sentence length, third A naive fence toggle counted shell commands as prose
    Prerequisites Two labels for one element

    Every one produced a number a reasonable person would have published. Five of the eight would have gone into this guide as findings.

    The instructional-design point is not "be careful". It is that the audit is an artifact with the same defect profile as the corpus it measures, and it needs the same treatment: a fixture with a known answer before you believe any number it gives you about something you care about.

    What you built. Four structural commitments, measured honestly, four pages repaired, one label standardised, and a calibrated distrust of your own first number.

    Reference¶

    Objectives in behavioural terms¶

    Weak Behavioural
    "Understand authentication" "Obtain a token and make an authenticated call"
    "Learn about retries" "Configure a retry policy and observe a backoff"
    "Get familiar with the CLI" "Install the CLI and list your widgets"
    "Know the architecture" "Trace one request through all three services"

    The test is whether you could watch someone and tell whether they had done it. "Understand" fails that test, which is why Intel's "to a working result" is better written than most objectives in actual curricula.

    Where expertise hurts¶

    Expert behaviour Consequence for the reader
    Omitting a step that is automatic for them A silent prerequisite
    Using the internal name for a thing Unfindable and unsearchable
    Explaining why before what The reader has nothing to attach it to
    Choosing the elegant path over the common one Correct and not useful
    Assuming the goal is obvious The reader does not know what they are building

    Cold-start testing is the mechanical defence against the first row. The others need a human reader, which is why assessment is a separate concept.

    What instructional design is not¶

    Not Because
    Making content friendlier Tone is not structure
    Adding more explanation Usually raises load
    A learning management system That is delivery
    Quizzes One kind of assessment, often the weakest
    Writing for beginners It applies at every level of expertise

    The commitments, and what each one costs¶

    Commitment Costs Buys
    Every page states its entry state One line, and a real decision Silent prerequisites become visible
    Every exercise states its duration One phrase Readers can decide whether to start
    Every exercise names its result Two sentences The author must know what it produced
    Every page says how to verify A section Separates informing from teaching
    One label per element A standardisation pass, once The commitments stay recognisable

    The last row is the one this page learned the hard way, and the one most corpora get wrong without noticing, because each author is individually consistent.

    How to talk about it¶

    Lead with the difference between the two jobs. "A reference page is done when it is accurate. A tutorial is done when a stranger finishes with a working result, which is a claim about the reader rather than the page. So I hold pages to structural commitments: declare the entry state, state the duration, name the result, say how to verify. Four things a script can check, which is why I chose those four."

    The follow-up is usually about expertise. The honest answer is that authors cannot see their own inferential leaps, so this needs mechanisms rather than care. Cold-start testing catches undeclared prerequisites; a required prerequisites line forces the author to decide what they are assuming; the rest needs a real reader, which is the limit of what tooling buys you.

    The tradeoff to volunteer. Structural commitments are a tax on every page and they can decay into ritual. A prerequisites line reading "none" on forty pages is noise, and a "what you built" paragraph written to satisfy a checker is worse than none at all. They work while they force a decision and fail once they become a form to fill in, and the only way to tell which has happened is to read what they produced.

    One sharper point. Auditing your own corpus is harder than it looks, and the failure is silent. Eight successive measurements of mine returned confident wrong numbers. Front matter counted as prose. A duration pattern defeated by line wrapping. A fence parser that counted shell commands as sentences. And two labels for one structural element, which looked like a quarter of the corpus failing a commitment it had already met. Five would have been published as findings. An audit script has the same defect profile as the corpus it measures, so it gets a fixture with a known answer before any of its numbers are believed.

    How to prove it¶

    The artifact. A corpus whose structural commitments are checked in CI, plus the audit and the fixtures that make the audit trustworthy.

    Three things to do, in order:

    1. Pick one commitment, audit a corpus you own for it, then verify the measurement by hand against three pages. The hand-check is the step people skip and the only one that catches a wrong number.
    2. Rewrite your three weakest learning objectives in behavioural terms. "Could I watch someone and tell?" is the entire test. The test is falsifiable or the objective is not rewritten: if you cannot name the observation that would show a reader had failed, the words changed and the objective did not.
    3. Find an exercise that never says what it produced, and write that sentence. It is the fastest way to discover an exercise that produces nothing.

    What makes it credible rather than decorative. fieldguide audit prints the four commitments on demand, which is the whole of the claim: a seven-section page contract enforced in CI, a prerequisite graph with no cycles, 89 of 89 prerequisite lines, and 106 of 106 golden paths stating a duration and naming a result. Every figure in this page's golden path comes from that command rather than from a transcript, and its test fixtures pin the three measurement bugs described above so they cannot come back.

    What that does not establish is the thing the concept is actually about. The commitments are met; whether meeting them teaches anybody is a claim about readers, and there are none. The demonstrated page records this as partial for that reason.
