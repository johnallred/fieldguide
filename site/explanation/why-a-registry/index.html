    Why the curriculum is a data file¶

    A checklist nobody can verify reports completion it has not earned, so this one is validated in CI.

    The obvious way to build this guide is a Markdown page with 106 checkboxes on it. Someone ticks one when they write a page.

    That version is worse than having no checklist, and it is worth being precise about why, because the same failure runs through most documentation projects.

    What a hand-maintained checklist actually reports¶

    A checkbox records an intention at the moment someone typed it. It does not record the state of the repository. Within a month the two have diverged in every direction at once:

    • A page was written and nobody ticked the box.
    • A box was ticked and the page is three paragraphs of TODO.
    • A page was renamed and the link under its ticked box is dead.
    • A concept turned out to be two concepts and the single box still says done.
    • Someone added a page for a concept that was never on the list.

    None of these are visible. The index page looks authoritative, which is precisely the problem: it is the page a reader trusts most and the page most likely to be wrong. A reader who follows a tick to a stub learns not to trust the index, and after that the index is decoration.

    What the registry does instead¶

    concepts.yaml holds the curriculum as structured data: 106 concepts with a category, a tier, a difficulty, a time estimate, prerequisites, the job posting line that justifies the concept, and a status.

    fieldguide check then refuses to let the registry and the repository disagree:

    The claim What catches a false one
    This concept is covered The page must exist, unless status is planned
    This page belongs here Every page must have a concept in the registry
    This concept is complete A complete page under 450 words is rejected
    This page follows the contract All seven required sections, in order
    This path is teachable No concept before a prerequisite the path also teaches
    This graph is followable No cycles, no dangling prerequisites
    A posting asks for this Every concept needs a source, provenance, and a quote

    The coverage matrix, the learning paths, and the full ordering are all generated from the registry. They are views, not copies, so there is no state in them that can drift. CI runs the generator with --check and fails if a committed view is stale, which closes the last gap: changing the registry without regenerating.

    It caught four real mistakes immediately¶

    This is the part that justifies the machinery rather than merely describing it.

    Two learning paths taught a prerequisite too late. The first-week path taught link checking before documentation CI, and the educator path taught reproducible environments before cold-start testing. Both were flagged on the first run.

    Both turned out to be wrong dependencies, not wrong orders. Thinking about it, you run a link checker on a tree long before you own a pipeline, so link checking depending on documentation CI was backwards. And a reproducible environment is how you make cold-start testing meaningful, so that edge pointed the wrong way too. The validator reported a symptom in the paths; the bug was in the model. That is the ordinary experience of working with a validated schema, and it is the argument for having one: the error surfaced somewhere cheap.

    The page-contract check and the stub check both found what they were built for while the first pages were being drafted.

    What it costs¶

    Honesty requires the other column.

    Adding a concept is now a two-step operation: edit the registry, then run the generator. A contributor who edits a generated page by hand loses the work. The banner on every generated page exists because of that, and a banner is a weaker guard than a lock.

    The schema has opinions, and some of them will be wrong. tier has three values and difficulty has three levels, which is a forced simplification of something continuous. A concept that is genuinely between core and supporting gets rounded, and the rounding is invisible to a reader.

    Eighty-three concepts in one flat namespace is near the limit of what this model carries well. There is no sub-categorisation and no notion of a concept that is a special case of another. At two hundred concepts this file would need to become a directory, and the generator would need to grow a notion of hierarchy it currently does not have.

    And a validated curriculum is still only one person's curriculum. Nothing here checks whether these are the right 106 concepts. The registry makes the list consistent and honest about its sources. It cannot make it correct, and no amount of schema will.
